# Riverside Public Library: Background Briefing

*Fictional example material, written for testing the Deliberation Partner.*

## The situation

The Riverside Public Library system runs three branches: Central, Northgate, and Elm Street. The town council has asked a panel of 24 randomly selected residents to recommend how the library should handle a 12 percent cut to its operating budget next year, which is roughly 410,000 dollars.

Staffing makes up about 70 percent of the library's operating costs. Materials (books, e-books, databases) make up about 15 percent. Buildings and utilities make up the remaining 15 percent.

## Who uses the library

In the last year the library recorded about 380,000 visits. Central accounts for just over half of them. Northgate is the busiest branch for children's programs. Elm Street is the smallest branch, but it is the only one within walking distance for many residents of the east side, where car ownership is lowest.

Use of e-books and audiobooks grew by about 30 percent over three years, while physical checkouts fell by about 8 percent over the same period. Computer and Wi-Fi use remains high at all branches, especially among job seekers and students.

## What the panel is asked to consider

The panel will consider four broad approaches, which can be combined:

1. Reducing opening hours across all branches.
2. Closing or consolidating one branch.
3. Shifting spending from physical materials toward digital materials.
4. Raising revenue through fees, partnerships, or a local levy that voters would need to approve.

Each approach affects different groups of residents in different ways. The panel is not asked to pick a single answer in advance, and there is no expected outcome.
