# Options and Trade-offs

*Fictional example material, written for testing the Deliberation Partner.*

## Option 1: Reduce hours

Cutting each branch by about 10 hours per week would save an estimated 250,000 dollars a year, mostly in staff costs. Supporters say this spreads the impact evenly and keeps every neighborhood served. Critics point out that evening and weekend hours are when working parents and students can visit, and those are often the hours cut first.

## Option 2: Close or consolidate a branch

Closing Elm Street would save an estimated 300,000 dollars a year. Supporters argue it protects hours at the two larger branches. Opponents note that Elm Street serves the east side, where residents are less likely to own a car, and that the nearest alternative branch is a 40 minute bus ride away.

## Option 3: Shift from physical to digital

Moving 40 percent of the physical materials budget to digital licenses would save little money directly, since digital licenses for libraries often cost more per title than print copies. It could, however, match how borrowing habits are changing. Concerns include residents without home internet or devices, and the fact that libraries license rather than own most e-books.

## Option 4: New revenue

A small local levy could close the gap entirely, but it requires a public vote and may not pass. Room rental fees and business partnerships could raise an estimated 30,000 to 60,000 dollars a year. Some residents worry that fees and sponsorship change what a public library is for.

## Questions panel members have raised

- How many people would actually be affected by each option?
- Can options be combined to spread the impact?
- What happened in other towns that faced similar choices?
